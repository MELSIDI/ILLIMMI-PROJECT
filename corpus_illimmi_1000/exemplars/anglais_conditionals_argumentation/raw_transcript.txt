Hello and welcome back! Today we are looking at two essential grammar and writing skills for exams: mastering conditional sentences and structuring persuasive paragraphs with the PEEL technique.

Conditionals often confuse students, but here is the secret:
- First conditional: Real possibilities in the future. "If you study consistently, you will pass your exams."
- Second conditional: Imaginary or hypothetical situations in the present. "If governments banned social media, teen anxiety would drop." Remember: past simple in the if-clause, would in the main clause.
- Third conditional: Past regrets that cannot be changed. "If we had listened to scientists earlier, climate impacts would have been mitigated."

When writing an essay, don't just state opinions. Use the PEEL method:
- Point: Your topic sentence.
- Evidence: Supporting facts, data, or quotes.
- Explanation: Analyze why this evidence supports your claim.
- Link: Tie your paragraph back to the overall essay thesis.

And remember the golden rule: never use "would" directly in the if-clause! It is always "If I had known", never "If I would have known".
