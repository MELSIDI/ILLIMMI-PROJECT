Hello everyone, and welcome to this English masterclass for high school students!
Today, we are going to unlock two game-changing tools to boost your written exams: mastering English conditionals and structuring top-tier argumentative essays using the PEEL method.

Look at the conditional timeline appearing on the board.
Too many students get confused between the three main conditionals. Let's make it crystal clear:
The First Conditional is about real possibilities in the future: If society regulates artificial intelligence, citizens will feel safer.
The Second Conditional explores hypothetical or unreal situations right now: If governments banned all social media tomorrow, mental health would probably improve. Notice that we use the past tense in the if-clause, but it talks about the present!
And the Third Conditional looks back at regrets in the past: If inventors had considered ethical hazards earlier, we would not have faced such deep digital divides today.

Now, how do you use these structures inside an actual essay? 
Look at the four building blocks of the PEEL method displayed in color:
P stands for Point: your clear topic sentence stating your central claim.
E stands for Evidence: a concrete real-world example, study, or cultural reference.
E stands for Explanation: the most important part, where you explain why and how your evidence proves your point.
And L stands for Link: the transition sentence connecting back to your overarching question.

Let's read a high-scoring paragraph example together:
First, our Point: If ethical frameworks were strictly enforced, algorithmic bias would decrease significantly.
Next, our Evidence: For instance, facial recognition tests conducted in 2024 revealed significant error rates on minority populations when unregulated.
Then, our Explanation: This demonstrates that technological neutrality is an illusion without deliberate oversight.
Finally, our Link: Therefore, responsible innovation must remain humanity's highest priority.

Beware of the number one mistake that examiners penalize in high school essays!
Never, ever put 'would' inside the if-clause! Saying 'if I would know' is a serious grammar error; you must say 'if I knew'.

Remember: conditionals give your arguments subtlety and depth, while PEEL ensures your thoughts are logically organized.
